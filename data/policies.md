# Residential Mortgage Underwriting Policy (synthetic)

This is a synthetic policy written for a software demo. It is not lending guidance and no real lender uses it.

Scope: a conventional loan to buy a single-unit home that the borrower will live in. Every period of time in this policy is counted back from the application date.

# 1 Credit

## 1.1 Credit score

The borrower needs a credit score of at least 620. The score also places the borrower in a tier, which the other sections refer to.

- 760 or higher: Excellent.
- 700 to 759: Good.
- 660 to 699: Fair.
- 620 to 659: Marginal. A Marginal file needs at least 2 of the compensating factors listed in 2.5.
- Below 620: not eligible.

## 1.2 Bankruptcy

A past bankruptcy is acceptable once enough time has passed since the court discharged it.

- Chapter 7: 4 years from the discharge date.
- Chapter 13: 2 years from the discharge date.
- A file inside the waiting period is not eligible.

## 1.3 Foreclosure

A past foreclosure is acceptable once 7 years have passed since it was completed. A file inside that period is not eligible.

## 1.4 Late payments

Recent payment history is judged on the last 12 months.

- No payment 60 or more days late in the last 12 months.
- Up to 2 payments that were 30 days late in the last 12 months are acceptable, and each one needs a letter of explanation (LOE) from the borrower.
- More than 2 such payments is a breach of this section.

## 1.5 Collections

Collection accounts are split into medical and non-medical.

- Medical collections are ignored.
- If non-medical collections add up to more than $2,000, they must be paid in full before closing.

# 2 Income

## 2.1 Work history

The borrower needs 2 years in the same line of work. Time with earlier employers counts when the work is of the same kind.

## 2.2 Employment gaps

Gaps in employment during the last 2 years must be explained.

- A gap longer than 30 days needs an LOE.
- After a gap longer than 6 months, the borrower must have been in the current job for at least 6 months.

## 2.3 Self-employed income

Self-employed income is taken from the net income of the 2 most recent years.

- Normal case: use the average of the 2 years.
- If net income fell by more than 20% from one year to the next, use the lower, more recent year, and flag the decline.

## 2.4 Bonus income

Bonus income counts only when it is established.

- It must have been received in each of the last 2 years, and the employer must expect it to continue.
- When it counts, use the average of the 2 years.
- Otherwise leave it out of qualifying income.

## 2.5 Debt-to-income ratios

Two ratios are measured against monthly qualifying income. The front-end ratio is the housing payment (PITI). The back-end ratio is PITI plus all other monthly debt payments.

- Front-end ratio: 28% or less.
- Back-end ratio: 43% or less.
- Exception: a back-end ratio above 43% and up to 50% is acceptable when the file has at least 2 of these compensating factors: a credit score of 720 or higher; post-closing reserves of 12 months or more; a loan-to-value ratio of 75% or less.
- A front-end ratio above 28% is a noted risk and needs the same 2 compensating factors.
- A back-end ratio above 50% is not acceptable.

# 3 Assets

## 3.1 Reserves

Reserves are the verified liquid funds left after closing, counted in months of PITI.

- Standard requirement: 2 months.
- Requirement of 6 months when any of these is true: the back-end ratio is above 43%; the borrower is self-employed; the credit score is below 680.

## 3.2 Funds to close

The down payment and closing costs must come from verified liquid funds: checking and savings balances, or a gift that is documented with a signed gift letter. An undocumented gift does not count.

## 3.3 Large deposits

A large deposit is any deposit, other than payroll, that is more than 50% of monthly qualifying income.

- Each large deposit needs documents that show where the money came from.
- Until it is documented, the amount is deducted from verified funds.

## 3.4 Retirement accounts

Balances in retirement accounts do not count toward funds to close or toward reserves.

# 4 Collateral

## 4.1 Value used for loan-to-value

Loan-to-value (LTV) is the loan amount divided by the lower of the purchase price and the appraised value.

## 4.2 Repairs

Repairs noted by the appraiser are sorted by kind.

- Health, safety or structural repairs must be finished before closing.
- Cosmetic repairs that total $5,000 or less may be covered by an escrow holdback and finished after closing.
- Cosmetic repairs above $5,000 must be finished before closing.

## 4.3 Maximum loan-to-value

- The maximum LTV is 95%.
- An LTV above 80% requires mortgage insurance. This is a requirement to note, not a reason to deny.

## 4.4 Property types

Eligible property types are single-family homes, townhouses and condominiums. Other types are not eligible.

## 4.5 Condominiums

A condominium requires a review of the condominium project before closing.

# 5 Decision and review

## 5.1 Risk score

Each file receives a risk score from 0 (lowest risk) to 100 (highest risk). The score sets the decision.

- 0 to 30: approve.
- 31 to 60: conditional approval, with the conditions listed.
- 61 to 100: deny, with the reasons stated.

## 5.2 Human review

A human underwriter must review the file when any of these applies.

- The decision is a conditional approval.
- The risk score is within 5 points of a threshold in 5.1, that is 26 to 35 or 56 to 65.
- The quality review of the analyses still reports a major defect when the decision is made.
- An analysis is missing or could not be completed.

## 5.3 Overrides

The human underwriter may change the decision. A decision that differs from the recommendation needs a written justification, which is kept with the file.
